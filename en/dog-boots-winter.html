Dog boots for winter: how to choose a pair your dog will actually keep on

Between de-icing salt, grit and freezing sidewalks, your dog's paw pads take a beating all winter. A good pair of boots solves most of the problem, as long as you pick the right size and type.

Why protect the paws

Salt and grit spread on sidewalks can irritate or dry out paw pads, and many dogs lick the residue once they're back home. Long-haired dogs also get snowballs stuck between their toes.

What to look for

  • A non-slip sole that is still flexible enough for the dog to feel the ground.
  • A snug closure at the ankle (wide hook-and-loop strap), ideally reflective for dark evenings.
  • A waterproof upper: wet snow soaks through thin fabric quickly.
  • A detailed size chart: measure the width of the paw while your dog is standing and compare it with the maker's chart.
  • Sets of 4: check whether your dog's front and back paws are the same size.

Sturdy boots or rubber booties?

Boots with a firm sole last several seasons and grip better on ice, but need a short adjustment period.

Thin rubber booties are cheaper and feel more natural, but they get lost and wear out faster.

Getting your dog used to them

  • Start indoors, a few minutes at a time, with treats.
  • Funny high-stepping at first is normal: most dogs adapt after a few walks.
  • Back home, rinse the paws with lukewarm water and dry them, boots or not.

FAQ

My dog refuses to walk in boots. Is that normal?

Yes, at first. Keep the sessions short, use treats, and check that the strap is neither too loose nor too tight.

How do I know the size is right?

The boot should not twist on the paw or slip off when you pull gently. If you're between two sizes, follow the maker's recommendation.

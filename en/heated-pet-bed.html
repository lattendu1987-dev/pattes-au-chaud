Heated pet bed or pad: how to choose one and use it safely

Senior cats, small dogs and pets that sleep near a window love a warmer bed in winter. There are two main types of heated pads: electric and self-warming. Here's how to choose.

Electric or self-warming?

  • Self-warming (no electricity): an insulating layer reflects your pet's body heat. No cord, no electrical risk: the best place to start.
  • Electric: more noticeable warmth, useful for a senior or recovering pet or a cold room. It needs more precautions.

What to look for (electric models)

  • Low wattage and gentle heat, close to your pet's body temperature.
  • Auto shut-off or a thermostat to prevent overheating.
  • A chew-resistant cord cover, especially with puppies or kittens.
  • A recognized safety certification and clear instructions.
  • A removable, washable cover.

Good habits

  • Always leave an unheated area next to it so your pet can move away if too warm.
  • Check the cord regularly and stop using it if it's damaged.
  • Ask your vet for advice for very young, very old or immobile pets.

FAQ

Can a heated pad stay plugged in overnight?

Follow the maker's instructions. A model with auto shut-off or a thermostat is preferable, and your pet should be able to move off the warm area.
